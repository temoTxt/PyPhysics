# Klein–Gordon equation (Klein 1926 + Gordon 1926)

*Skeleton bib note.* Independent derivations by Oskar Klein and Walter Gordon (and also Schrödinger himself, earlier in 1926, in unpublished work) of the relativistic wave equation $(\Box + m^2c^2/\hbar^2)\psi = 0$ for a scalar charged particle. Two infamous problems: (i) negative-energy solutions, (ii) the conserved current $j^0 \propto \psi^* \partial_t \psi - (\partial_t \psi^*) \psi$ is *not* positive-definite, so it cannot be interpreted as a probability density. **The dual-framework resolution** (`#verified` via the verification campaign): [[FoundationsII-Classical]]'s positive-definite Hamiltonian $K = H^2/(2mc^2) + mc^2/2$ has no negative-energy modes, and the quantum-mechanical extension via [[Analytic_Representation_of_The_Square-Root_Operator]] gives a single-particle relativistic theory with positive-definite probability density. The negative-probability problem doesn't arise — that's the **headline conceptual payoff** of the dual framework for this era. Animated in `hist_klein_gordon_vs_dual.py`.
