# Über die Bewegung der Elektrizität in Drähten

*Skeleton bib note. Body to be filled in during PR C's QA pass.*

Kirchhoff's analysis of telegraph-cable signal propagation, in which (independently of the Weber–Kohlrausch 1856 ratio-of-units measurement) the velocity of electric disturbance on a wire turns out to equal — within experimental error — the speed of light in vacuum. The constant `c` makes its first appearance in an electromagnetic context *not* connected to optics. This is the empirical fact Maxwell will pick up and amplify in *On Faraday's Lines of Force* (1855–56) and the 1865 *Dynamical Theory*: electromagnetism *is* optics. Kirchhoff himself didn't make the optical leap explicitly — that's Maxwell's contribution in Ch 2.

`#inferred` flag: the appearance of `c` from the ratio of esu/emu units carries forward intact into the dual framework as the limit `b → c` at low velocities; the algebraic structure of Kirchhoff's telegrapher equation is unchanged by the substitution `t → τ` because at 1850s signal speeds the proper-time correction is below detection.
