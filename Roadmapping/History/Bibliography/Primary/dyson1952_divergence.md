# Divergence of Perturbation Theory in Quantum Electrodynamics

*Skeleton bib note.* Dyson's heuristic argument that the QED perturbation series in $\alpha$ is *asymptotic, not convergent*: a hypothetical replacement $\alpha \to -\alpha$ would correspond to a universe with same-sign-attracting electrons — energetically unstable — so the series cannot have a finite radius of convergence around $\alpha = 0$. The **Dyson divergence conjecture**. Practical QED computations still give correct numbers to 12+ digits because the *asymptotic* series is dominated by the first few terms at $\alpha \approx 1/137$. The dual framework's resolution: Gill's [[Feynman_Operator_Calculus_Papers|Feynman Operator Calculus]] line of work organises the time-ordered operator algebra on a Kuelbs–Steadman (KS) Hilbert space that *bypasses* the resummation problem. The Dyson divergence becomes a feature of one specific representation, not a structural barrier. Crossed-reference verified in the campaign as the load-bearing motivation for Gill's *Foundations II Classical* and *Foundations I Mathematical* construction.
