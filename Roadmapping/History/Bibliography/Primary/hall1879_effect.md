# On a New Action of the Magnet on Electric Currents

*Skeleton bib note. Body to be filled in during PR D's QA pass.*

Hall, a graduate student at Johns Hopkins (supervised by Henry Rowland), shows that a current-carrying thin gold leaf in a transverse magnetic field develops a *transverse* potential difference perpendicular to both the current and the field. The discovery of what becomes the Hall effect. Sign of the Hall voltage in metals depends on whether the charge carriers are positive or negative — a discriminator that won't be properly used until the 1920s solid-state work, but the methodological foundation is here. The first major solid-state result tying to electromagnetism, opening the long line that leads to band theory (Bloch 1928), the transistor (1947), and the quantum Hall effect (1980). `#gill-silent` — pure quasi-static magnetostatics.
