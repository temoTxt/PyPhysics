# The Scattering of α and β Particles by Matter and the Structure of the Atom

*Skeleton bib note.* Rutherford's interpretation of the Geiger–Marsden gold-foil scattering experiments. The observed back-scattering of alpha particles (a small fraction reflected almost backward) is incompatible with Thomson's "plum pudding" atom; Rutherford's small, dense, positively-charged nucleus reproduces the angular distribution exactly. The nuclear atom. Sets up the Bohr model two years later (which must explain why orbiting electrons don't radiate themselves into the nucleus) and the entirety of nuclear physics — including the binding-energy curve that drives fusion ([[09_fusion_open_questions|Chapter 9]]).
