# Experimental Researches in Electricity, First Series

*Skeleton bib note. Body to be filled in during PR C's QA pass.*

The famous "induction ring" experiment of August 1831, published the following March: two coils wound on opposite sides of a soft-iron ring; the act of closing the circuit on the primary coil induces a momentary current in the secondary. Faraday calls this "magneto-electric induction" — the inverse of Ørsted's effect. Sections 1–58 of this paper introduce the concept of *induced* currents, the role of *change* (not steady state) in the inducing field, and the rotating-disc dynamo demonstration (sec. 90, the "Faraday disc"). The mathematical form $\mathcal{E} = -d\Phi/dt$ is implicit in Faraday's prose; Maxwell formalizes it in 1855–1865.

`#inferred` flag is forward-looking: the standard induction law extrapolates to the dual framework's Maxwell Eq. (3′) trivially in the quasi-static regime (`b ≈ c`), with no measurable deviation at experimental velocities Faraday could achieve.
