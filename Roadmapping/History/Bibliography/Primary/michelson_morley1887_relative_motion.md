# On the Relative Motion of the Earth and the Luminiferous Ether

*Skeleton bib note. Body to be filled in during PR D's QA pass.*

The decisive null result. Michelson and Morley's interferometer — two perpendicular arms ~11 m each, multiply folded by mirrors, mounted on a stone slab floating on mercury — should have shown a fringe shift of ~0.4 fringes between two orthogonal orientations if the Earth moved through a stationary luminiferous aether at orbital velocity (~30 km/s). The observed upper bound is ~0.01 fringes. The result is incompatible with a stationary aether at any orbital speed. Resolved (separately) by Fitzgerald (1889) and Lorentz (1892) via length-contraction, by Einstein (1905) via Special Relativity, and — by the route this campaign cares about — by Gill's dual framework, in which the collaborative speed $b = \sqrt{c^2 + u^2}$ provides a natural framing of the null result *without* invoking length contraction or Lorentz transformations of length. The proper-time framing absorbs the result into the relation between observer time $t$ and proper time $\tau$. See [[02_classical_synthesis_1860_1900#3. Proper-time commentary]] for the dual-framework derivation.
