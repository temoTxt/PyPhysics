# Electrodynamics from Ampère to Einstein

*Skeleton bib note. Body to be filled in during PR C's QA pass.*

The modern academic reference for 19th-century electromagnetism history, replacing Whittaker (1910) as the standard scholarly account. Darrigol's interpretive thesis is that two distinct *traditions* — the Continental action-at-a-distance tradition (Ampère, Weber, Neumann, Helmholtz) and the British field tradition (Faraday, Thomson, Maxwell) — coexisted and competed until the Hertz experiments (1888) and Lorentz's electron theory (1892–95) effectively settled matters in favor of the field view. Detailed coverage of the period 1820–1860 (Chs 1–3) directly relevant to our Ch 1; later chapters feed into Ch 2.
