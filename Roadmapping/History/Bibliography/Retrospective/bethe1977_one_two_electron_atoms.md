# Quantum Mechanics of One- and Two-Electron Atoms

*Skeleton bib note. Body to be filled in during the Bethe–Salpeter campaign's PR A QA pass.*

The canonical reference for precision atomic spectroscopy: one-electron (hydrogen, hydrogenic ions) and two-electron (helium, helium-like ions) atoms treated under standard QM and QED. First published Plenum 1957; the Springer 1977 reprint preserves the original section + equation numbering and is the edition cited throughout the [Bethe–Salpeter precision-predictions campaign](../../../Quantum_Mechanics/Bethe_Salpeter/) (issue [#50](https://github.com/temoTxt/PyPhysics/issues/50)).

**Used in the campaign as the precision-experiment counterpart to [[griffiths2018_qm_3e]]**: where Griffiths demonstrates pedagogical reduction of the proper-time `K` to non-relativistic Schrödinger, Bethe–Salpeter is the textbook where the proper-time formulation has measurable consequences — Lamb shift (§§19–21, measured `1057.845(9)` MHz), hydrogen hyperfine structure (§22, measured `1420.405751…` MHz), fine structure of H (§14), and helium correlation energies (§§47–60).

## Why this textbook earns a dedicated campaign

Bethe–Salpeter is encyclopedic in a way Griffiths is not: ~400 sections covering everything from the Schrödinger spectrum of hydrogen through the Bethe (1947) mass-renormalisation estimate of the Lamb shift through helium variational calculations. The campaign triages to the ~30–50 results that are *precision-comparable* — where modern measurement is sufficient to distinguish the proper-time formulation's predictions from textbook QED.

The headline payoffs are at PR C (§14 fine structure), PR E (§§19–21 Lamb shift), and PR F (§22 hyperfine structure). These are the campaign's experimental discriminators.
