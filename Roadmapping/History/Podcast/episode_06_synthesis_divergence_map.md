## Category 1: Identical predictions

**Physicist:** Most of the historical record falls here. Whenever the source is at rest in the lab frame — Faraday's coils, Hertz's antenna at typical lab velocities, the photoelectric apparatus, Lamb's microwave resonator — the source velocity $u$ is zero or negligible, the collaborative speed $b = \sqrt{c^2 + u^2}$ reduces to $c$ exactly, and the dual Maxwell, dual Dirac, and dual classical Hamiltonian reduce to their standard counterparts identically.

**Experimentalist:** Which is to say: the entire experimental record from 1800 to 1860 is a null comparison. Most of 1860 to 1900 is too. Bohr's hydrogen spectrum, Compton scattering off free electrons at rest, Schrödinger's non-relativistic atom, Dirac's fine structure, the Lamb shift, the anomalous magnetic moment — all of these are predicted identically by the standard and dual framings, at any precision experiments could measure.

**Physicist:** Right. The dual framework doesn't *contradict* standard physics. It would have been a much smaller campaign if it did.

## Category 2: Divergence below current precision

**Experimentalist:** Where standard and dual predictions *do* differ numerically, but the difference is smaller than current experiments can measure.

**Physicist:** Five concrete cases. Michelson–Morley fringe shift: both framings predict zero; the dual framework just gets there without invoking length contraction. Bohr hydrogen spectrum: relative divergence of order $v^4/c^4 \sim 10^{-9}$ for hydrogen orbital velocities; current hydrogen spectroscopy reaches $10^{-14}$, but at that precision QED radiative corrections dominate in both framings equivalently. Compton scattering off a bound electron at $u/c \sim 10^{-2}$: relative divergence $\sim 10^{-4}$; current atomic Compton-profile measurements reach $10^{-3}$, barely below. GPS satellite clocks at orbital $u/c \sim 10^{-5}$: divergence $\sim 10^{-10}$; current GPS precision is $\sim 10^{-9}$, marginal. Fusion plasma cross-sections: divergence $\sim 10^{-4}$; current ICF precision is two orders of magnitude worse.

**Experimentalist:** So the experimental regime where the standard and dual framings could be empirically distinguished sits *just beyond* current measurements in some cases — GPS clocks, atomic Compton profiles — and well beyond in others.

**Physicist:** Right. The dual framework is *currently* equivalent to standard physics in its predictions. Future experiments at the next precision frontier — say, optical-lattice clocks an order of magnitude better than current GPS clocks — might in principle distinguish them.

**Experimentalist:** That's a lot of physics.

**Physicist:** It is. The dual framework is mostly a reformulation of *kinematics and dynamics in flat spacetime*. The high-energy gauge structures and the curved-spacetime physics sit outside what's been published. The chapter is honest about this in §4.

## Category 4: The three load-bearing interpretive wins

**Physicist:** The cases where the dual framework reframes a known structural difficulty without changing predictions. These are the campaign's central conceptual payoffs.

**Experimentalist:** Three of them.

**Physicist:** First: Michelson–Morley without length contraction. Standard SR resolves the 1887 null result by introducing length contraction as a kinematic postulate. The dual framework's velocity duality — $\mathbf{w}/c = \mathbf{u}/b$, equation (1) of Gill's Maxwell paper — already encodes the frame-dependent kinematics needed to give the null. Length contraction falls out as a consequence rather than being introduced as an independent postulate. Identical predictions. Cleaner conceptual structure.

**Experimentalist:** Second?

**Physicist:** Klein–Gordon's negative-probability problem dissolved. Standard quantum mechanics handles relativistic single-particle states by going to Dirac — first-order in time, 4-spinor structure, positive-definite probability density built in, then negative-energy modes absorbed via hole theory. The dual framework's positive-definite Hamiltonian $K = H^2/(2 m c^2) + m c^2/2$ from Gill's Foundations II Classical paper plus the square-root operator construction gives a single-particle relativistic theory with manifestly positive probability density *without* the spinor doubling. Antimatter is then organised via Santilli isodual companion equations rather than as Pauli-filled negative-energy seas. Same observable predictions for the positron. Different mathematical organisation.

**Experimentalist:** And the third?

**Physicist:** Dyson's 1952 divergence conjecture, reframed. The standard argument — analytically continuing $\alpha \to -\alpha$ gives an unstable theory, so the series cannot have a finite radius of convergence — relies on a specific representation of the QED operator algebra. Gill's Foundations I Mathematical paper organises the same algebra on a Kuelbs–Steadman Hilbert space, with the time-ordered Feynman Operator Calculus providing the perturbative scaffolding. In that representation the resummation problem is dissolved structurally. Same observable predictions: twelve-digit agreement on the electron $g-2$, on the Lamb shift. Different mathematical foundations.

**Experimentalist:** None of which changes what current QED measurements observe.

**Physicist:** Exactly. The interpretive wins live at the level of mathematical organisation. The campaign's predictions and the standard predictions coincide at any current precision.

## The three flagged findings

**Historian:** And the campaign's most concrete deliverable: the three internal-consistency findings flagged for author review. These are *not* claims that the dual framework is wrong — they are places where the algebra in the cited papers doesn't reproduce.

**Physicist:** First. Maxwell paper equation 24. Missing factor of $c$ in the spin-magnetic-field term $e\hbar \boldsymbol{\Sigma}\cdot\mathbf{B}/(2m)$ — should be over $2mc$, not $2m$. And a missing term $+V^2/(2mc^2)$. Two typos in the same equation. Fixable as written.

**Experimentalist:** Second. DRQM I equation III.22 — the headline payoff. The dual Dirac eigenvalue problem yields a formula for the electron g-factor; the published value of the internal parameter $r$ gives $g \approx -2.0006$, not the experimental $-2.0023$. The corrected $r$ — computed via the verification campaign's Wolfram MCP runs — is $r \approx 0.499420510\,r_0$, where the paper had stated $\approx 0.499857\,r_0$. The formula is correct; the parameter value stated in the paper is wrong.

**Physicist:** Third. TCEP equation 4.16. The paper writes the group-velocity relation $v_g = v_g' - v$; the algebra in the paper, and the paper's own commentary in the surrounding paragraph, give $v_g = v_g' + v$. Sign typo.

**Physicist:** Right. The "findings for author review" framing makes the channel clean: reproducible results computed via Wolfram MCP, with explicit corrected values, ready to take back to the original authors for response.
