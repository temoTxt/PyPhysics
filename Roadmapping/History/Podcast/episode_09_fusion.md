## Historical sweep

**Historian:** The arc. 1905 — Einstein writes $E = mc^2$. 1920 — [[aston1922_mass_defect|Aston's mass-spectrograph]] shows that helium-4 weighs less than two protons + two neutrons. The mass defect, multiplied by $c^2$, is enormous — about 28 MeV per helium nucleus, or 0.7% of the rest mass. [[eddington1920_stellar_fusion|Eddington in 1920 conjectures]] that this is what powers stars. His famous line: "What is possible in the Cavendish Laboratory may not be too difficult in the Sun."

**Physicist:** [[bethe1939_ppchain|Bethe in 1939]] works out the quantitative mechanism. The proton-proton chain dominates in cool low-mass stars like the Sun; the CNO cycle dominates in hot high-mass stars. The first step of the pp-chain — proton plus proton fuses to deuterium plus positron plus neutrino — is governed by the weak interaction, and that's *why the Sun's lifetime is gigayears rather than seconds*. The weak coupling is small enough that the average proton waits about 10 billion years before it fuses with another. Nobel 1967.

**Historian:** Then the wartime work — Manhattan Project's fission programme leads naturally into a fusion programme. The Teller–Ulam thermonuclear design in 1951 — classified, military, weapons-only. Civilian controlled fusion starts in 1957 with [[lawson1957_criterion|John Lawson's criterion]] for a power-producing thermonuclear reactor. Lawson's calculation gives the minimum confinement product $n\tau \gtrsim 10^{20}$ particles per cubic meter per second at $\sim 10$ kiloelectronvolts.

**Physicist:** Sixty-five years pass between Lawson and the first laboratory demonstration of ignition. Many false starts. T-3 in Russia in 1958 introduces the tokamak. JET in the UK operates from 1983 through 2024 and holds the magnetic-confinement record at 69 megajoules of fusion energy in a 5-second pulse at gain 0.33. NIF in the US, designed primarily for stockpile-stewardship simulations of thermonuclear weapons, achieves ignition as a side benefit in 2022.

**Historian:** And the path to grid-scale fusion electricity?

**Physicist:** ITER in France targets Q=10 around 2039. Commonwealth Fusion Systems targets Q>2 with high-temperature-superconductor magnets by 2027. Private fusion has raised about $6 billion across multiple companies. The technical questions are well-defined — materials, tritium breeding, disruption avoidance, repetition-rate IFE — and decades from solved.

## Speculative proper-time implications

For ICF imploded cores at $u/c \sim 3 \times 10^{-3}$, the dual correction to the barrier height is order $5 \times 10^{-6}$. Below current capsule-diagnostics precision by orders of magnitude. The conjecture: dual EM predicts slightly modified Coulomb barriers; the prediction is unmeasurable at current precision.

**Historian:** Second?

**Physicist:** Modified Lorentz force in plasma. Maxwell paper equation 18 has a $V/(mcb)$ correction term. For typical fusion plasma ions, $V/(mc^2) \sim 10^{-6}$ — 10 keV thermal vs MeV rest mass. The relative force correction is order $10^{-8}$, vs MHD modelling errors of order $10^{-2}$. Below detection by many orders.

**Historian:** And the third?
