\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

We present here three framework-specification questions that have proved
necessary across the three parallel candidate investigations of a
first-principles derivation of \(r_e/r_0\). Each question, if resolved,
would permit a derivation that goes beyond the algebraic inheritance
from QED's \(a_e(\alpha)\) that the present closed-form result
expresses.

\hypertarget{what-the-three-investigations-converged-on}{%
\subsection{What the three investigations converged
on}\label{what-the-three-investigations-converged-on}}

Candidate 3 confirmed, by a line-by-line reading of §III.D Eqs.
(III.18)--(III.23)\footnote{Candidate 3: issue \#66 and PR \#70,
  \texttt{github.com/temoTxt/PyPhysics/issues/66},
  \texttt{github.com/temoTxt/PyPhysics/pull/70}. Includes the
  line-by-line read of DRQM I §III.D Eqs. (III.18)--(III.23) and the
  FNAL Muon \(g-2\) 2023 cross-particle test (iter-5).}, that the
as-published apparatus leaves \(r_e/r_0\) as a free parameter. The
algebraic-back-fit match to the closed form

\[
\frac{r_e}{r_0} \;=\; \frac{2 - \alpha/(2\pi)}{4 + \alpha/\pi}
\tag{1}
\]

is necessary but not sufficient for intentional Schwinger encoding. A
cross-particle test against the FNAL Muon \(g-2\) 2023 measurement,
performed in the same investigation, yielded

\[
\frac{r_\mu}{r_0^\mu} \;-\; \frac{r_e}{r_0^e} \;=\; 3.13 \times 10^{-6}
\tag{2}
\]

at roughly \(57{,}000\,\sigma_{a_\mu}\). It follows that any universal
closed form \(r/r_0 = f(\alpha)\) in \(\alpha\) alone is empirically
excluded.

Candidate 2 found that the published expanded \(K_D\) of Eq. (III.4) is
structurally inadequate to pin \(r_e\) via the variational
principle\footnote{Candidate 2: issue \#65 and PR \#69,
  \texttt{github.com/temoTxt/PyPhysics/issues/65},
  \texttt{github.com/temoTxt/PyPhysics/pull/69}. Includes the iter-4
  diagnostic on the NR-expansion / cutoff-scale tension.}. No scale
exists where both (a) the NR expansion is valid and (b) the cutoff
couples meaningfully to the closure equation. At the electron-radius
scale (\(\hat r_e \sim 1\)) the kinetic-energy term dominates by four
orders of magnitude; the NR expansion is therefore invalid. At the Bohr
scale the expansion is valid, but the cutoff \(\hat r_e\) is suppressed
by \(\alpha^2\), and the closure matches plain hydrogen to ten
significant figures.

Candidate 1 gave the closed form

\[
\frac{r_e}{r_0} \;=\; \frac{2 - a_e}{2(2 + a_e)}
\tag{3}
\]

matching the triangulated value to \(3.45 \times 10^{-13}\)\footnote{Candidate
  1: issue \#64 and PR \#72,
  \texttt{github.com/temoTxt/PyPhysics/issues/64},
  \texttt{github.com/temoTxt/PyPhysics/pull/72}. Closed-form derivation
  of \(r_e/r_0\) by algebraic inversion of Eq. (III.22) of DRQM I,
  evaluated at QED's \(a_e(\alpha)\).}. Equation (3) is the algebraic
inverse of the framework's existing \(g\)-formula (Eq. III.22) evaluated
at QED's \(a_e(\alpha)\). This corresponds to
reproduction-by-inheritance under hypothesis (ii), in which the photon
propagator is unchanged and all dual structure is absorbed into the
(II.3) Pauli source. We observe that, on this reading, the framework
does not independently re-derive Schwinger's vertex result; it inherits
it.

The convergent gap across all three candidates is a first-principles
derivation of \(a_e\) from framework-internal dynamics. Such a
derivation requires three framework specifications that the published
apparatus does not currently supply. We do not ask the authors to
perform the derivation; rather, we ask whether the framework, as
intended, specifies (or implicitly fixes) the three pieces below. Each
admits a brief answer.

\hypertarget{the-three-questions}{%
\subsection{The three questions}\label{the-three-questions}}

\hypertarget{the-proper-time-photon-propagator-d_ftaux-y}{%
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{1. The proper-time photon propagator
\(D_F^{(\tau)}(x-y)\)}{1. The proper-time photon propagator D\_F\^{}\{(\textbackslash tau)\}(x-y)}}\label{the-proper-time-photon-propagator-d_ftaux-y}}

Two natural readings emerge from the framework's existing structure. The
first is Schwinger proper-time with \(b\)-dispersion,

\[
k^2 \;=\; (\omega/b)^2 \;-\; \mathbf{k}^2,
\qquad
b \;=\; \sqrt{c^2 + \mathbf{u}^2},
\tag{4}
\]

in which the photon dispersion inherits \(b\)-factors from the
dual-current structure
\(J^\mu_{\rm Gill} = (b\rho,\,\mathbf{J})\)\footnote{Dual current
  \(J^\mu_{\rm Gill} = (b\rho,\,\mathbf{J})\): Gill--Zachary, \emph{Two
  Mathematically Equivalent Versions of Maxwell's Equations}, Eq. (12),
  in
  \texttt{Roadmapping/Converted\_Markdown/Two\_Mathematically\_Equivalent\_Versions\_of\_Maxwells\_Equations/}.}.
The second is the standard Feynman propagator,

\[
k^2 \;=\; (\omega/c)^2 \;-\; \mathbf{k}^2,
\tag{5}
\]

with the \(b/c\) conversion absorbed into the source's coupling rather
than into the propagator, and the dual structure entering through
modified vertex factors. The two readings give different numerical
\(r_e/r_0\) at the same order in \(\alpha\). At the \(\log(b/c)\) level
they are discriminable at the framework's precision. It is not clear to
us which (if either) is the intended choice.

\hypertarget{the-bound-state-propagator-g_cxye-in-the-ii.3-potential-in-the-mass-kernel}{%
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{2. The bound-state propagator
\(G_C(x,y;E)\) in the (II.3) ``potential-in-the-mass''
kernel}{2. The bound-state propagator G\_C(x,y;E) in the (II.3) ``potential-in-the-mass'' kernel}}\label{the-bound-state-propagator-g_cxye-in-the-ii.3-potential-in-the-mass-kernel}}

For the bound-electron one-loop self-energy, one requires a propagator
that handles the Coulomb potential non-perturbatively at the cutoff
scale, where the NR expansion of Eq. (III.4) is invalid (per Candidate
2's diagnostic). Standard QED uses the Furry-picture Coulomb-bound
propagator, summing the external Coulomb potential to all orders in
\(Z\alpha\) before perturbing in \(\alpha\). It is not clear from the
published apparatus whether the framework specifies (or implicitly uses)
a dual analog of the Furry-picture construction, or some other treatment
of the bound-state propagator under the (II.3) kernel.

\hypertarget{the-mass-renormalisation-prescription-at-the-cutoff}{%
\subsubsection{3. The mass-renormalisation prescription at the
cutoff}\label{the-mass-renormalisation-prescription-at-the-cutoff}}

The natural framework-internal closure is

\[
\langle K_D \rangle_{r_e}
\;=\; m_e c^2 \;+\; \Delta E_{\rm bind}^{\rm framework} \;+\; \Delta E_{\rm SE}^{\rm framework}.
\tag{6}
\]

The textbook NR working hypothesis
(\(\Delta E_{\rm bind} = \langle V_0\rangle\),
\(\Delta E_{\rm SE} = 0\)) gives a trivially-zero closure under the
published expanded \(K_D\), per Candidate 2's diagnostic. We seek the
framework's intended form for \(\Delta E_{\rm SE}^{\rm framework}(r_e)\)
at the cutoff scale. The relevant analog is QED's on-shell mass
renormalisation condition,

\[
m_{\rm phys} \;=\; m \;+\; \Sigma(p)\big|_{p^2 = m^2 c^2},
\tag{7}
\]

now to be transposed into the dual framework's proper-time formulation.

\hypertarget{posture}{%
\subsection{Posture}\label{posture}}

Any one of these would advance the corresponding candidate; all three
together would permit the full hypothesis-(i) calculation. The thread is
non-urgent. The triangulated value

\[
r_e/r_0 \;=\; 0.499\,420\,509\,912\,831\,7
\tag{8}
\]

already serves as the campaign's current best refinement at the
framework's precision floor\footnote{Triangulation note:
  \texttt{Roadmapping/Author\_Reports/2026-05\_re\_triangulation\_followup\_for\_gill.md}.}.
It is consistent with the framework's apparatus at every observable in
the joint fit. The substantive content of the three questions is whether
the framework, as intended, contains the specifications that would close
the gap from empirical cutoff to framework-derived quantity. The
alternative is that closing that gap is genuinely new theory work the
published apparatus does not yet support.

If the answer to any of (1), (2), (3) is that the framework does not yet
specify the relevant piece, that is itself a substantive result. It
would mean the campaign's terminal verdict on \(r_e/r_0\) should be
recorded as \textbf{Branch B, QED-inheritance, pending future framework
development}, rather than as \textbf{Branch A, from first principles}.
Either outcome closes the question honestly; what is at stake here is
which honest framing is the right one.

The corresponding responses will be recorded on the
framework-specifications issue\footnote{Issue \#75
  (framework-specifications),
  \texttt{github.com/temoTxt/PyPhysics/issues/75}.}, and the derivation
work resumed once the specifications are in hand.
