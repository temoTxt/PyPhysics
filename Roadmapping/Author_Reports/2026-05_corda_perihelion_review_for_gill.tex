The paper's two thesis claims appear verbatim in §9. The first is that
it is not correct that Newtonian theory cannot predict the anomalous
rate of precession of the perihelion of planets' orbits; the real
problem is instead that a pure Newtonian prediction is too large. The
second is that the perihelion's precession can be achieved with the same
precision as general relativity by extending Newtonian gravity through
the inclusion of gravitational and rotational time-dilation effects.

The math and physical-intuition check below addresses each thesis
separately.

\subsection{§2. Every load-bearing equation surfaced and

We state Corda's equations verbatim (translated from the marker-pdf
conversion), record the Wolfram-MCP-verified algebra, and flag the

\hypertarget{eqs.-35-circular-orbit-newton-baseline-no-back-reaction}{%
\subsubsection{§2.1 Eqs. (3)--(5) --- circular-orbit Newton baseline (no
back-reaction)}\label{eqs.-35-circular-orbit-newton-baseline-no-back-reaction}}

\[
\frac{G M m}{r_{0}^{2}} \;=\; \frac{m\,v_{0}^{2}}{r_{0}}, \tag{3}
\]

\[
v_{0} \;=\; \sqrt{G M / r_{0}}, \tag{4}
\]

\[
T_{0} \;=\; \frac{2\pi\,r_{0}}{v_{0}} \;=\; \frac{2\pi\,r_{0}^{3/2}}{\sqrt{G M}}. \tag{5}
\]

These are standard textbook expressions (Newton with centripetal
balance). The algebra is correct. The physical content is that Mercury
orbits a stationary Sun. \textbf{Pass.}

\hypertarget{eqs.-67-newtonian-angular-velocity-and-unperturbed-angle}{%
\subsubsection{§2.2 Eqs. (6)--(7) --- Newtonian angular velocity and
unperturbed
angle}\label{eqs.-67-newtonian-angular-velocity-and-unperturbed-angle}}

\[
\omega_{0} \;=\; 2\pi / T_{0}, \tag{6}
\]

\[
\varphi_{0} \;=\; \omega_{0}\,T_{0} \;=\; 2\pi. \tag{7}
\]

These are definitionally correct. \textbf{Pass.}

\hypertarget{eqs.-813-the-substitution-that-drives-cordas-2-result}{%
\subsubsection{§2.3 Eqs. (8)--(13) --- the substitution that drives
Corda's §2
result}\label{eqs.-813-the-substitution-that-drives-cordas-2-result}}

Corda's core substitution is to replace \texttt{M} in (3) with
\texttt{M\ +\ m}, on the ground that the Sun-centred observer sees
Mercury's reaction force on the Sun:

\[
\frac{G(M+m)\,m}{r_{0}^{2}} \;=\; \frac{m\,v^{2}}{r_{0}}. \tag{8}
\]

He justifies the substitution by writing the Newton's-third-law pair:

\[
\vec F_{G} \;=\; \frac{G M m}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\hat u_{r}, \tag{9}
\]

\[
M\,a_{s}\,\hat u_{r} \;=\; \frac{G M m}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\hat u_{r}
\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;
a_{s} \;=\; \frac{G m}{r_{0}^{2}}, \tag{10}
\]

\[
m\,a_{m}\,\hat u_{r} \;=\; -\,\frac{G M m}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\hat u_{r}
\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;
a_{m} \;=\; -\,\frac{G M}{r_{0}^{2}}, \tag{11}
\]

\[
a\,\hat u_{r} \;\equiv\; a_{m} - a_{s} \;=\; -\,\frac{G(M+m)}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\hat u_{r}, \tag{12}
\]

\[
F\,\hat u_{r} \;=\; -\,\frac{G(M+m)\,m}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\hat u_{r}. \tag{13}
\]

The algebra of (8)--(13) is correct. It is the standard two-body
relative-acceleration result. The same \texttt{G(M\ +\ m)} appears in
textbook reduced-mass treatments (Goldstein §3.1, MTW §25). What
\texttt{(M\ +\ m)} captures is the kinematic fact that the
\emph{relative} acceleration of Mercury with respect to the Sun is
bigger than \texttt{GM/r²} by the factor
\texttt{(M\ +\ m)/M\ =\ 1\ +\ m/M}. \textbf{Pass on algebra.}

\textbf{Physical-intuition status, for the author's verdict.} What is
being computed is the \emph{relative} acceleration in the centre-of-mass
frame; it is not a Sun-fixed force on Mercury. The standard reduced-mass
two-body decomposition replaces the two-body problem with a one-body
problem for a reduced-mass particle \texttt{μ\ =\ Mm/(M\ +\ m)} orbiting
a fixed centre at \texttt{M\ +\ m}. Corda's Eq. (12) is correct. The
interpretation in §2, in which the substitution is read as a
``perturbation'' to Mercury's force, muddles the standard story.

\hypertarget{eqs.-1417-perturbed-velocity-and-period}{%
\subsubsection{§2.4 Eqs. (14)--(17) --- perturbed velocity and
period}\label{eqs.-1417-perturbed-velocity-and-period}}

\[
v \;=\; \sqrt{G(M+m)/r_{0}}, \tag{14}
\]

\[
T \;=\; \frac{2\pi\,r_{0}}{v} \;=\; \frac{2\pi\,r_{0}^{3/2}}{\sqrt{G(M+m)}}, \tag{15}
\]

\[
(M+m)^{-1/2} \;=\; M^{-1/2}\,(1 + m/M)^{-1/2}, \tag{16}
\]

\[
T \;=\; T_{0}\,(1 + m/M)^{-1/2}. \tag{17}
\]

The algebra of (17) is verified by Wolfram MCP\footnote{Wolfram MCP
  verification: the residual \texttt{T\ −\ T₀/√(1\ +\ m/M)} for
  \texttt{T\ =\ 2π\ r₀/v}, \texttt{v\ =\ √(G(M\ +\ m)/r₀)},
  \texttt{T₀\ =\ 2π\ r₀\^{}(3/2)/√(GM)} simplifies to \texttt{0}.}: the
residual \texttt{T\ −\ T₀/√(1\ +\ m/M)} simplifies to \texttt{0}. The
period decreases slightly because the effective gravitational constant
in the one-body problem is \texttt{G(M\ +\ m)}, not \texttt{GM}. This is
a real kinematic effect on the sidereal period. \textbf{Pass on
algebra.}

\hypertarget{eqs.-1820-angular-velocity-and-perihelion-advance}{%
\subsubsection{§2.5 Eqs. (18)--(20) --- angular velocity and
``perihelion
advance''}\label{eqs.-1820-angular-velocity-and-perihelion-advance}}

\[
\omega \;=\; \omega_{0}\,(1 + m/M)^{1/2}, \tag{18}
\]

\[
\varphi \;=\; \omega\,T_{0} \;=\; 2\pi\,(1 + m/M)^{1/2}
\;\simeq\; 2\pi\,(1 + m/(2M)), \tag{19}
\]

\[
\Delta\varphi \;=\; \varphi - \varphi_{0} \;\simeq\; \pi\,m/M. \tag{20}
\]

The Taylor expansion is verified by Wolfram MCP\footnote{Wolfram MCP
  verification:
  \texttt{Series{[}2π\ √(1\ +\ x)\ −\ 2π,\ \{x,\ 0,\ 2\}{]}} returns
  \texttt{π\ x\ −\ π\ x²/4\ +\ O(x³)}; the leading term is
  \texttt{π\ m/M}.}:
\texttt{Series{[}2π\ √(1\ +\ x)\ −\ 2π,\ \{x,\ 0,\ 2\}{]}} returns
\texttt{π\ x\ −\ π\ x²/4\ +\ O(x³)}, with leading term \texttt{π\ m/M}.
Numerically for Mercury (\texttt{m/M\ =\ 1.66\ ×\ 10⁻⁷}), one obtains

\[
\Delta\varphi \;=\; 5.21 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{rad/orbit}
\;\times\; 415.2\,\text{orbits/century}
\;\times\; \tfrac{180 \cdot 3600}{\pi}
\;=\; 44.66\,\text{arcsec/century}. \tag{R1}
\]

The arithmetic is Wolfram-verified. \textbf{Pass on algebra.}

\textbf{Physical-intuition status, for the author's verdict --- the
load-bearing critique.} What Eq. (20) computes is the angle Mercury
sweeps in the \emph{unperturbed} period \texttt{T\_0} at the
\emph{perturbed} angular velocity \texttt{ω}. This quantity is not a
perihelion advance. The perihelion advance is the angular rotation of
the orbit's major axis between successive perihelia. It is a property of
the \emph{shape} of the orbit, not of the period or angular velocity.

For an inverse-square force, the orbit is a closed ellipse (Bertrand's
theorem). The angle between successive perihelia is therefore exactly
\texttt{2π}, irrespective of \texttt{T} and \texttt{ω}. Changing
\texttt{G} from \texttt{GM} to \texttt{G(M\ +\ m)} rescales the orbital
speed and the period, but it leaves the orbit a closed ellipse. The
\texttt{πm/M} quantity computed in (20) is a real kinematic effect; it
is not the perihelion-advance quantity. The §3 derivation makes this
explicit; we return to it in §2.7.

\hypertarget{eqs.-2131-cordas-own-apsidal-angle-formalism-the-papers-3}{%
\subsubsection{§2.6 Eqs. (21)--(31) --- Corda's own apsidal-angle
formalism (the paper's
§3)}\label{eqs.-2131-cordas-own-apsidal-angle-formalism-the-papers-3}}

Following Price and Rush (1979), Corda writes the radial equation,
imposes angular-momentum conservation, Taylor-expands around
\texttt{r\_0}, and arrives at the classical Bertrand-style apsidal
angle:

\[
F_{c0}(r) \;=\; m\,\bigl(\ddot r - \omega_{0}^{2}\,r\bigr), \tag{21}
\]

\[
J_{0} \;=\; m\,r^{2}\,\omega_{0}, \tag{22}
\]

\[
F_{c0}(r) \;=\; m\,\Bigl(\ddot r \;-\; \frac{J_{0}^{2}}{m^{2}\,r^{3}}\Bigr), \tag{23}
\]

\[
F_{c0}(r_{0}) \;=\; -\,\frac{J_{0}^{2}}{m\,r_{0}^{3}}. \tag{24}
\]

After perturbing \texttt{x\ =\ r\ −\ r\_0} and series-expanding,

\[
\ddot x \;+\; m^{-1}\!\left[\,-\,\frac{3\,F_{c0}(r_{0})}{r_{0}} \;-\; F'_{c0}(r_{0})\,\right] x \;=\; 0, \tag{27}
\]

\[
T_{0} \;=\; 2\pi\!\left(\frac{m}{-\,3\,F_{c0}(r_{0})/r_{0} \;-\; F'_{c0}(r_{0})}\right)^{1/2}, \tag{28}
\]

\[
\frac{\varphi_{0}}{2}
\;=\; \pi\!\left(\frac{m}{-\,3\,F_{c0}/r_{0} \;-\; F'_{c0}}\right)^{1/2}\,\frac{J_{0}}{m\,r_{0}^{2}}. \tag{29}
\]

Using (24) in (29):

\[
\varphi_{0}
\;=\; 2\pi\!\left[\,3 \;+\; \frac{F'_{c0}(r_{0})\,r_{0}}{F_{c0}(r_{0})}\,\right]^{-1/2}. \tag{31}
\]

We verify (31) for \texttt{F\ =\ −k/r²} symbolically in
Wolfram\footnote{Wolfram MCP verification: with
  \texttt{F{[}r{]}\ :=\ −kk/r²}, the ratio
  \texttt{F\textquotesingle{}(r)\ r\ /\ F(r)} at \texttt{r\ =\ r₀} is
  \texttt{−2}, and \texttt{φ\_0\ =\ 2π/√(3\ +\ (−2))\ =\ 2π}. The
  inverse-square force gives no precession.}: the ratio
\texttt{F\textquotesingle{}(r)\ r\ /\ F(r)} evaluates to \texttt{−2},
and the apsidal angle becomes \texttt{φ\_0\ =\ 2π/√(3\ +\ (−2))\ =\ 2π}.
Indeed, for an inverse-square force the apsidal angle is \texttt{2π},
the orbit is closed, and there is no precession. This is precisely
Bertrand's theorem at work.

The algebra of (21)--(31) is correct, and this conclusion contradicts
the §2 reading. Corda derives the apsidal-angle formula from scratch in
his Eq. (31), and specialises immediately afterwards to the
inverse-square case \texttt{F\_\{c0\}\ =\ F\_G}. He records there that
the apsidal angle reduces to \texttt{φ\_0\ =\ 2π}, the same value as his
own Eq. (7), and notes explicitly that this is the closed-orbit result.
\textbf{Pass on algebra; the conflict is with the §2 interpretation.}

\hypertarget{eqs.-3241-cordas-promotion-to-the-1-mm-force}{%
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{§2.7 Eqs. (32)--(41) --- Corda's
``promotion'' to the \texttt{(1\ +\ m/M)}
force}{§2.7 Eqs. (32)--(41) --- Corda's ``promotion'' to the (1 + m/M) force}}\label{eqs.-3241-cordas-promotion-to-the-1-mm-force}}

To extend the apsidal-angle calculation to the reduced-mass case, Corda
substitutes the central force law:

\[
F_{c0}(r) \;\to\; F_{c}(r) \;=\; \Bigl(1 + \frac{m}{M}\Bigr)\,F_{c0}(r), \tag{32}
\]

\[
\omega_{0} \;\to\; \omega, \tag{33}
\]

\[
J_{0} \;\to\; J \;=\; m\,r^{2}\,\omega. \tag{34}
\]

This is meant to mirror the §2 substitution \texttt{M\ →\ M\ +\ m}
inside the central-force law. Then

\[
F'_{c}(r_{0}) \;=\; \Bigl(1 + \frac{m}{M}\Bigr)\,F'_{c0}(r_{0}), \tag{36}
\]

\[
\frac{F'_{c}(r_{0})}{F_{c}(r_{0})}
\;=\; \frac{F'_{c0}(r_{0})}{F_{c0}(r_{0})}. \tag{37}
\]

Equation (37) is verified by Wolfram\footnote{Wolfram MCP verification:
  with \texttt{Fc{[}r{]}\ :=\ (1\ +\ mMerc/Msun)\ Fc0{[}r{]}}, the
  expression
  \texttt{Fc\textquotesingle{}(r₀)/Fc(r₀)\ −\ Fc0\textquotesingle{}(r₀)/Fc0(r₀)}
  simplifies to \texttt{0}.}: the residual
\texttt{F\textquotesingle{}\_c/F\_c\ −\ F\textquotesingle{}\_\{c0\}/F\_\{c0\}}
simplifies to \texttt{0}, since the \texttt{(1\ +\ m/M)} factor cancels
in \texttt{F\textquotesingle{}/F}. \textbf{Pass on algebra.}

Here Corda's own apparatus contradicts his own §2 conclusion. The
apsidal-angle formula (31) depends \emph{only} on the ratio
\texttt{F\textquotesingle{}/F}. Equation (37) says that constant
rescaling of \texttt{F\_\{c0\}} leaves \texttt{F\textquotesingle{}/F}
unchanged. Therefore the apsidal angle for the rescaled force
\texttt{F\_c\ =\ (1\ +\ m/M)\textbackslash{},F\_\{c0\}} is the
\emph{same} as for the unscaled force \texttt{F\_\{c0\}}, which is
\texttt{2π} (from Eq. 31 with \texttt{F\_\{c0\}\ =\ F\_G\ =\ −k/r²}).
The ``perturbed'' force still gives a closed orbit. There is no
precession.

Corda's subsequent Eqs. (38)--(41) derive
\texttt{T\ =\ T\_0\ (1\ +\ m/M)\^{}(−1/2)} again from the period formula
(28), then \texttt{ω\ =\ ω\_0\ (1\ +\ m/M)\^{}(1/2)}, then
\texttt{φ\ =\ ωT\_0\ ≈\ 2π(1\ +\ m/(2M))} --- the same \texttt{πm/M} as
§2. This is exactly the §2 mistake repeated: a period change rephrased
as a perihelion advance. Corda's own Eq. (31), applied to his own Eq.
(32) force, contradicts the §3 conclusion. \textbf{Algebra pass;
interpretation refuted by the apparatus.}

\hypertarget{eqs.-4257-reduced-mass-kepler-derivation}{%
\subsubsection{§2.8 Eqs. (42)--(57) --- reduced-mass Kepler
derivation}\label{eqs.-4257-reduced-mass-kepler-derivation}}

Corda's §4 (``Third Kepler's law'') works through the standard
centre-of-mass plus reduced-mass two-body decomposition:

\[
\vec R \;=\; \frac{m\,\vec r_{m} + M\,\vec r_{M}}{M + m},
\qquad
\vec r \;=\; \vec r_{m} - \vec r_{M}, \tag{43}
\]

\[

\hypertarget{the-load-bearing-critique-three-findings}{%

\hypertarget{finding-1-algebra-driven.-bertrands-theorem-rules-out-corda-24}{%
\subsubsection{Finding 1 (algebra-driven). Bertrand's theorem rules out
Corda
§§2--4}\label{finding-1-algebra-driven.-bertrands-theorem-rules-out-corda-24}}

Corda's apsidal-angle formula (Eq. 31), applied to \emph{any}
inverse-square force --- including the \texttt{(1\ +\ m/M)}-rescaled
version of Eq. (32) --- gives \texttt{φ\_0\ =\ 2π} exactly. Indeed, for
\texttt{F\ =\ −k/r²} one has
\texttt{F\textquotesingle{}(r)\ r\ /\ F(r)\ =\ −2}, and so
\texttt{φ\_0\ =\ 2π/√(3\ −\ 2)\ =\ 2π}. For
\texttt{F\ =\ (1\ +\ m/M)(−k/r²)} one has the same
\texttt{F\textquotesingle{}/F\ =\ −2/r}, and so the same
\texttt{φ\_0\ =\ 2π}.

This is Bertrand's theorem in action. Of all central potentials, only
\texttt{1/r} (Kepler, Newton) and \texttt{r²} (harmonic) give
universally closed bound orbits. Inverse-square forces (Newton's
gravity, Eq. 9) produce closed ellipses with no precession, at any
energy or angular momentum. Constant rescaling of an inverse-square
force does not change this; it just changes the orbital period.

It follows that Corda's Eq. (20), \texttt{Δφ\ =\ πm/M}, is not an
apsidal precession. It is the angle by which Mercury's position differs
from \texttt{2π} when measured at time \texttt{T\_0} (the unperturbed
period) under the perturbed angular velocity \texttt{ω}. After the true
perturbed period \texttt{T\ =\ T\_0(1\ +\ m/M)\^{}(−1/2)} has elapsed,
Mercury has swept exactly \texttt{2π} again and has returned to
perihelion. \textbf{No precession. Refuted.}

\hypertarget{finding-2-empirics-driven.-the-5-self-refutation}{%
\subsubsection{Finding 2 (empirics-driven). The §5
self-refutation}\label{finding-2-empirics-driven.-the-5-self-refutation}}

Applying Corda's own formula \texttt{Δφ\ =\ π\ m/M\ ×\ (orbits/century)}
to Venus and Earth gives the numbers tabulated in §2.10 above; from (R4)
and (R5), the formula predicts \texttt{257.91} arcsec/century for Venus
against the observed \texttt{8.62}, and \texttt{194.50} arcsec/century
for Earth against the observed \texttt{3.83}. The Mercury ``agreement''
of \texttt{44.66} against \texttt{43} is the lone case in which the
formula and the data line up.

Corda's response, that n-body effects are responsible and that Mercury
has no interior planets, does not work as a defence. The formula
\texttt{Δφ\ =\ πm/M} makes no reference to n-body effects. The
derivation in §§2--4 is strictly two-body throughout, with no interior
planets entering. If the formula were the correct two-body result, it
would apply to any two-body sub-problem, and the n-body corrections
would be smaller perturbations rather than discrepancies by a factor of
\texttt{30} to \texttt{50}.

\textbf{For the author's verdict.} The strongest available evidence that
\texttt{πm/M} is not the correct two-body perihelion advance is that the
formula is empirically wrong for every planet other than Mercury. The
Mercury ``agreement'' is coincidence: the value
\texttt{m/M\ ≈\ 1.66\ ×\ 10⁻⁷}, multiplied by Mercury's \texttt{415.2}
orbits/century, happens to give a number near \texttt{43}
arcsec/century. Venus and Earth have different
\texttt{(m/M,\ orbits/century)} products and give wildly wrong
predictions. \textbf{Refuted.}

\hypertarget{finding-3-formal-derivation-driven.-68-reproduce-only-the-e-0-gr-limit}{%
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Finding 3 (formal-derivation-driven).
§§6--8 reproduce only the \texttt{e\ =\ 0} GR
limit}{Finding 3 (formal-derivation-driven). §§6--8 reproduce only the e = 0 GR limit}}\label{finding-3-formal-derivation-driven.-68-reproduce-only-the-e-0-gr-limit}}

Corda's Eq. (77), \texttt{Δφ\_F\ =\ 3π\ r\_g/r\_0}, is correct algebra
(modulo the §2.13 typographical error) but recovers only the
circular-orbit limit of the true GR result
\texttt{3π\ r\_g/{[}a\ (1\ −\ e²){]}}. For Mercury (\texttt{e\ =\ 0.21})
this is a \texttt{4.4\%} shortfall (\texttt{−1.82} arcsec/century).

\textbf{For the author's verdict.} §§6--8 is a real first-order GR
derivation route. What it gets right is the ``PPN sum of gravitational
and Doppler time dilation'' result, well known in GR pedagogy. What it
does not capture is the eccentricity dependence, which makes it
numerically equivalent to GR only for nearly circular orbits. For
Mercury the eccentricity correction matters; for Venus and Earth
(smaller \texttt{e}), the expression \texttt{3π\ r\_g/a} is much closer
to the full GR answer.

The two §§6--8 results work in their domain. The trouble is purely the
combination with §§2--4. Corda writes in §9 that ``Newtonian plus
corrections matches GR.'' The honest reading is that §§6--8 \emph{alone}
matches GR (in the circular limit); §§2--4 contributes a fictitious
\texttt{πm/M} that does not correspond to a real precession; and the two
together do not add up to GR --- the \texttt{πm/M} is simply spurious.
\textbf{Marginal on §§6--8 by themselves; refuted on the §9 combination
claim.}

\hypertarget{implication-for-relativistic-newtonian-theory}{%

\emph{interpretation} of correctly derived algebra. The report does not
claim that \emph{Relativistic Newtonian Theory} as a whole is wrong; the

deliver a Mercury-matching prediction; the framework may have
alternative extensions to gravity beyond the one analysed in
\emph{Relativistic Newtonian Theory}, and this report only addresses the
specific extension that paper proposes. The report does not claim that
the apsidal-angle critique is novel; it is straightforward textbook

framework and to \emph{Relativistic Newtonian Theory} as an inheritance
