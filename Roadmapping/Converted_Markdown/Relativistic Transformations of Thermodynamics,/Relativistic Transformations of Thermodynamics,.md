#### 6 Concluding Remarks

The Proper Time in Redefined Relativistic Thermodynamics has been defined and therefore for each particle system if we can calculate or describe the motion of the canonical center of mass described by Eq. (52), we can physically describe the entire thermodynamics of a system in equilibrium. Many applications can be made from this result. Indeed, in the Introduction, we note that it can be understood from Redefined Relativistic Thermodynamics that the frame of reference such that the blackbody carrying the 2.7K background radiation is at rest corresponds to the Hydra-Centaur direction with a speed of 627 km/sec [18]. Also, the cases where two non-interacting gases with different temperatures collide can be easily solved by Redefined Relativistic Thermodynamics [44]. Other examples can be cited, but in all of them a measurement system has to be defined. However, Einstein's dual theory defines the center of mass of any interacting system that obtains equilibrium regardless of whether the particles interact or not. That is: in each proposal the rest frame of the system can be defined excluding the ideas of the Non-Interacting Theorem.

Therefore, the three proposals can be used to deal with a system of particles. First, if we want to deal with the volume that represents all the events that simultaneously occur in the rest frame of the volume but if we look at this volume from the frame K (the measuring devices), the ROG proposal is the right one. This method has been used by Gamba to clarify the electromagnetic controversy of the 4/3term [43], [32]. Second, when all the points of the volume are simultaneous in a reference frame K but the measuring devices are in a frame with velocity u with respect to the frame K, the PE proposal describes the thermodynamics of the system, even more so when it is a system composed of several gases that collide at different temperatures and reach equilibrium [44]. Third, the L proposal seems to be the most adequate technique to deal with a thermodynamic system which is at rest with the measuring devices. Therefore, for any system that can reach thermodynamic equilibrium, part of the problem is reduced to obtaining or calculating the canonical dual Hamiltonian center of mas of Einstein's Dual Theory.
