## Per-paper evaluation

### 1. [[cheung2025_strings_from_almost_nothing]] — "Strings from Almost Nothing"

**Summary.** Cheung, Remmen, Sciotti, and Tarquini prove that the Veneziano (planar 4-point) and Virasoro-Shapiro (nonplanar 4-point) amplitudes are the *unique* tree-level, crossing-symmetric, Lorentz-invariant, unitary amplitudes satisfying two minimal conditions: (i) **ultrasoftness** — the leading Regge trajectory α(t) is bijective for t < 0 and α(t) → −∞ as t → −∞, so the amplitude falls off arbitrarily fast at large momentum transfer; and (ii) **minimal zeros** — every zero of every residue is a "Regge zero" mandated by consistency (the technical statement: residues vanish at α(t) + r = 0 for positive integers r, an infinite set of consistency-mandated zeros that follows from the big-arc integral analysis in their Eqs. (3)–(6)). Their argument extends to nonplanar 4-point (giving Virasoro-Shapiro uniquely) and to 5-point scattering. The authors explicitly disclaim experimental relevance: *"experiment — the ultimate arbiter of all theories — will not offer any insight into this question in the foreseeable future, or perhaps ever."*

**Technical fidelity check.** The big-arc-integral derivation (Eqs. 3–6) is a clean application of standard Regge-limit machinery. The key new ingredient is the **minimal-zeros** assumption (ii): they explicitly note this is their "least conservative condition" but show that it sharpens an earlier attempt (Ref. [88]) which relied on a *universal* zero-distribution conjecture later shown to be violated. The result — linear spectrum μ(n) ~ J(n) ~ n + linear Regge trajectory α(t) ~ t + the "level truncation" ansatz of Refs. [91, 92] — uniquely picks out the Veneziano gamma-function ratio. The proof structure is internally consistent on a careful read; the only step that looks like it could hide a loophole is the discrete-to-continuous approximation in Eq. (5), which the authors acknowledge holds in the Regge limit |t| ≪ μ(n) with μ'(n) ≪ μ(n) — both standard for the family of amplitudes under study.

**Verdict on Zorns-Lemmon's axis: brilliant** (with the caveat that "brilliant" here means *technically rigorous and novel within scope*, not "settles the empirical question of string theory"). Authors are well-credentialed (Cheung at Caltech / Walter Burke; Remmen at NYU / James Arthur Fellowship; Sciotti at IFAE / BIST; Tarquini at Caltech); paper sits in an active and productive sub-literature (S-matrix bootstrap, EFT positivity, primal bootstrap — Refs. [1]–[94] are largely from 2014–2025); the result addresses a genuinely open question that earlier work (Ref. [88]) only partially answered.

**Relevance to the Gill program: tangential.** Gill's framework modifies single-particle relativistic kinematics (the b/c factors in modified Lorentz transformations / dual proper time / the r_e/r_0 critical-point thread in [`FoundationsII-Classical.md`](../../FoundationsII-Classical.md) Sec 2.2.1). Cheung et al. constrain *tree-level 4- and 5-point scattering amplitudes* via S-matrix bootstrap. These live at different layers: amplitudes are derived objects in a quantum field theory built on a fixed Lorentz structure; Gill modifies the Lorentz structure itself. No anchor in any Equation_Verification doc maps to a Regge-zero structure or a 4-point amplitude residue. **No direct overlap.** The methodological lesson — derive a unique theory from minimal axioms — is the substantive point of the forwarding comment and is handled below in §3.

**Provenance signals.** arXiv preprint, August 2025, not yet (as of read date 2026-05-24) appeared in a journal that I confirmed via the converted markdown. References [1]–[94] are dominated by S-matrix-bootstrap / positivity-bound community names (Caron-Huot, Penedones, Vieira, Arkani-Hamed, Maldacena, Zhiboedov). The author team has a sustained track record in the area (multiple self-references including Refs. [4], [14], [16], [23], [24]). Funding acknowledgments are normal (DOE DE-SC0011632, Walter Burke Institute, NextGenerationEU). No red flags.

### 2. [[qunigudzinas2026_invariant_geometric_qm]] — "Quantum-Mechanical Physics as Invariant Geometric Structure"

**Summary.** Quni-Gudzinas observes that the fine-structure constant equals the ratio of the classical electron radius to the reduced Compton wavelength, α = r_e / λ̄_C ≈ 1/137.036, and frames this ratio as a projective cross-ratio (0, ∞; r_e, λ̄_C) invariant under Möbius transformations. The author then proposes a "Type I / Type II / Type III" classification of the Standard Model's ~26 dimensionless constants based on whether the underlying Compton frequency cancels (Type I — universal couplings), exposes a single mass ratio (Type II — e.g. m_p/m_e), or combines scales from different sectors (Type III — e.g. λ̄_C^(e)/ℓ_P). A 600-combination computational scan reports a null result for non-tautological Type-II ratios and several Type-III near-matches within 5% (notably λ_top/λ_e ≈ y_e within 0.93%). Number-theoretic extensions are sketched (adelic product formula, Bruhat-Tits tree, p-adic absolute values) and explicitly flagged as speculative. The author states verbatim: *"The framework makes no novel quantitative predictions. Its contribution is organizational and programmatic."*

**Technical fidelity check.** The core identity is algebraically trivial and known: r_e = e²/(4πε₀ m_e c²) and λ̄_C = ħ/(m_e c) give r_e/λ̄_C = e²/(4πε₀ ħ c) = α by direct substitution. The "cross-ratio" framing (0, ∞; r_e, λ̄_C) is *technically* a valid use of the cross-ratio formula but degenerate — with two of the four points fixed at 0 and ∞, the cross-ratio reduces to r_e/λ̄_C, which is what one started with. The "invariance under Möbius transformations" claim is therefore vacuous as physics content: r_e and λ̄_C are dimensional length scales, not freely transformable projective coordinates. The 600-combination scan is honest (the null result for Type-II ratios is correctly reported; the λ_top/λ_e ≈ y_e near-match follows from the well-known coincidence m_top ≈ v/√2). The number-theoretic sections (adelic product, Bruhat-Tits tree as "geometry of frequency space") cite the author's own prior work (*Tree of Frequencies*, *One Pattern*, *Adelic Cross-Ratio*) rather than independent literature, and the author is careful to flag them as conjectural ("if fundamental dimensionless constants are rational... [a hypothesis, not an established fact]").

**Verdict on Zorns-Lemmon's axis: mundane** (with speculative extensions). The core observation is a textbook identity dressed in projective-geometry language that adds no new mathematical content. The Type I/II/III classification is genuinely useful pedagogy but not novel — the distinction between frequency-cancelling and frequency-exposing ratios is implicit in standard dimensional-analysis treatments. The number-theoretic extensions are *speculative* in the author's own classification (his §14.2) and rest on prior unpublished work by the same author. Not crackpot — the author is meticulous about labeling claims as established vs. speculative, and the 600-combination null result is reported honestly even when it disfavors the program's most ambitious aspiration (deriving mass ratios from cross-ratios) — but not brilliant either. This sits firmly in the "interesting framing of known content, with speculative extensions whose predictions are deferred to other documents" category.

**Relevance to the Gill program: tangential.** Both Gill and Quni-Gudzinas work with "geometric" ratios of length scales, so there is a surface-level kinship. But Gill's r_e/r_0 critical-point claim (Maxwell paper Open Q3, resolved per [`FoundationsII-Classical.md`](../../FoundationsII-Classical.md) Sec 2.2.1) is about a *specific dynamical* feature of the dual-relativistic framework, not a general classification of dimensionless constants. The Quni-Gudzinas classification doesn't predict, constrain, or comment on Gill's b/c modifications. The author's prior-work program (Tree of Frequencies, Adelic Constraints, MASS-FREQUENCY) appears to be an entirely separate research thread that does not engage Gill's literature. **No direct overlap.** Caveat: the author's cite-able predictions (CMB log-periodic oscillations, qubit decoherence oscillations, lepton-universality of α) are entirely outside the Gill framework's scope and would not be confirming or disconfirming evidence for it.

**Provenance signals.** Single-author Zenodo deposit, May 2026, not peer-reviewed. PDF was exported from Obsidian (visible in PDF metadata via `pdfinfo`). The document's references chain — *Tree of Frequencies* (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.20071716), *One Pattern* (0.23.md), *Adelic Cross-Ratio*, *Number Theory as Physics*, *MASS-FREQUENCY project* — all appear to be the author's own prior outputs (the source-cross-reference table in Appendix B lists no independent citations). The author has an ORCID (0009-0002-4317-5604) and ISNI, but the ORCID prefix `0009-` indicates a relatively recent registration (post-2022). No university affiliation is listed. This is solo, non-peer-reviewed work outside mainstream physics-publishing channels. That doesn't make the content wrong — but it shifts where the verification burden lies (a reviewer must do more independent work, since the author's prior corpus is not externally vetted).

## Methodological engagement — bootstrap as derivational tool for Gill's b/c modifications

Zorns-Lemmon's forwarding comment raised this directly: *"It did get me thinking about the bootstrapping approach, which seems to be at least somewhat in vogue, and might be something to apply here."* This is the substantive value of his comment and is engaged here as the plan's §5.4 decision required.

**Where the analogy holds.** The Cheung et al. spirit is: start from a few physically motivated axioms (unitarity, locality, Lorentz invariance, ultrasoftness, minimality of zeros) and *derive* a uniquely specified theory rather than postulating its form. This is a healthy methodological move and applies broadly across theoretical physics — Lovelock's theorem deriving GR from {diffeomorphism invariance + 2nd-order equations + low-energy correspondence + dimension} is the classic analogue. The *intent* of the bootstrap program — "show that what looks like a free choice is actually forced" — is exactly the kind of move that would strengthen the Gill framework if it could be made to work. A bootstrap-style derivation of the b/c modifications from a small set of principles would replace the current "the dual proper-time geometry implies …" presentation (which reads as *postulation*) with "the following axioms uniquely imply the b/c-modified Lorentz transformation."

**Where the analogy breaks.** Cheung et al.'s specific technical machinery — Mandelstam variables s, t, u; the Regge limit |t| ≪ μ(n); residue zeros at α(t) + r = 0; the level-truncation ansatz — operates on the *S-matrix* (tree-level 2→2 and 2→3 scattering amplitudes) of a quantum field theory built on a *fixed* Lorentz background. Gill's modifications are *to* the Lorentz background, not derived objects on top of one. Concretely:

- A Cheung-style bootstrap takes Poincaré invariance as input; Gill's program changes the invariance group itself, so this input is the *thing* to be derived, not an axiom.
- Residue structures of 4-point amplitudes don't constrain single-particle proper-time geometries. The bootstrap apparatus has no observable to push against Gill's b/c factors.
- The "uniqueness" claim in Cheung et al. is uniqueness *up to* the input axioms. For a Gill-style bootstrap to be analogous, the input axioms would need to be a *different* set entirely — likely something like {a modified Poincaré-group-style invariance + low-velocity correspondence with standard SR + a maximality / extremality principle on proper time + ?}.

**What a Gill-specific adaptation would actually look like.** A defensible research program would attempt: derive the b/c-modified Lorentz transformation as the *unique* one-parameter deformation of the standard Lorentz transformation satisfying {(a) reduction to standard SR in some limit; (b) preservation of a specified invariant quantity, e.g. a modified proper-time interval; (c) some additional axiom — perhaps a maximality condition on proper time along timelike worldlines, or compatibility with a specified energy-momentum dispersion relation}. The technical machinery would be Lie-algebraic deformation theory (closer to the literature on doubly special relativity / κ-Poincaré algebras) rather than amplitude bootstrap. The Cheung et al. paper supplies useful *motivation* — "minimal-axiom derivations are how physics-of-record gets built today" — but not transferable *technique*.

**Bottom line.** Worth pursuing as a follow-up direction (see §Candidate follow-up actions below), but not as a literal application of the Cheung et al. machinery. The right citation when opening that line of work would be the κ-Poincaré / doubly-special-relativity literature plus Cheung et al. as a methodological referent, *not* Cheung et al. as a technical template.
